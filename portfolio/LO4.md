## 4. Evaluate limitations of the testing process, using statistical methods where appropriate

**4.1 Gaps and omissions.** *Mutation testing* was not run, so the number of faults still hidden in the game is not estimated; I had to leave it out for lack of time. Other techniques planned in LO2 and not done: *pairwise* testing of bot inputs, property-based tests and *MC/DC* on the betting conditions. Betting checks that refuse a folded or all-in player, and the error paths in `pot`, have no tests. Win probability is checked exactly only against one opponent, so accuracy against eight is untested. No real person has played the game, so there is no *usability* or *acceptance* testing and no *operational profile*. Bot-only tournaments stand in for real play, and they finish only by using fast random equity.

**4.2 Targets and motivation.** In the test plan, before measuring anything, I set *branch coverage* targets by risk: 95% for `card`, `deck`, `player`, `pot`, `betting` and `evaluator`, where a mistake gives the wrong winner or wrong chips; 90% for `probability`; 85% for `game`; 80% for `bot`, whose choices are heuristic and only need to be legal. Other targets are no fault left open at critical or severe level, every requirement passing, and 2,000 simulations in under 1 s. Coverage is only an adequacy proxy, so these numbers say where to look, not whether the suite is good.

**4.3 Achieved against target.** `Card` (97%) meets 95%. `Deck` (92%), `betting` (92%) and `evaluator` (91%) are a few points under; `game` (82%) is 3 points under 85%. `Player` (78%) and `pot` (77%) are well under 95%, and `bot` (56%) and `probability` (42%) are far from 80% and 90%. The one line missed in `deck` is its empty-deck error, which play cannot reach. The largest shortfalls come from unsatisfiable obligations: an older bot decision method and hand-type tables used only for display are never called, so no test could run them. I report the extent reached (the slides' second approach), which counts them as missed; excluding them would raise both figures. The rest of the shortfall is the untested refusals and `pot` error paths. Timing exceeds its target: 0.22 s for 2,000 simulations. The statistical evidence is weaker than it looks. All 16 detectable injected faults were caught (17 injected; one cannot be observed), but 16 of 17 supports only a 95% interval of about 71% to 100% for the suite's detection rate. They were chosen by me, and I found no real fault, so the *seeding* ratio cannot estimate how many real faults remain.

**4.4 What reaching the targets needs.** Delete the unused code, or test it; that alone should lift `bot` and `probability` most. Add tests for the betting refusals and `pot` errors, but only if the game can reach them, which I judged not to be worth the time. Run mutation testing with operators such as relational-operator and constant replacement on `evaluator`, `pot` and `betting`, and kill live mutants, treating equivalent ones separately; sampling mutants (*statistical mutation*) would keep the cost down. Then the detection rate comes from hundreds of mutants, not 17. Finally, run a short *usability* session with three to five players, and log real hands to build an operational profile for reliability estimates.
